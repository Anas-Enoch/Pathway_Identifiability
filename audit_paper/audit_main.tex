\title[Identifiability of independent sampling units in public metabolomics data]{Identifiability of independent
sampling units in publicly deposited metabolomics data: a repository-level audit and documentary assessment}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Anas} \sur{Enoch}}\email{anas\_nour@um5.ac.ma}
\affil*[1]{\orgname{Mohammed VI University of Sciences and Health (UM6SS)}, \orgaddress{\city{Casablanca}, \country{Morocco}}}

\abstract{\textbf{Introduction.} Public metabolomics repositories enable secondary analysis and external validation, but
valid inference requires that independent sampling units be identifiable. Unique sample labels do not identify
distinct participants. \textbf{Objectives.} To assess whether repository metadata and linked documentation establish
participant-level sampling structure under prespecified, affirmative evidence rules. \textbf{Methods.} A metadata-only
census of 3,582 public MetaboLights accessions; a secondary audit of the factor metadata of 104 previously selected
Metabolomics Workbench studies; a randomised documentary assessment of 40 of 371 unresolved MetaboLights candidates,
reading deposit metadata together with linked publications; and a pre-verification of 19 human deposits whose rows an
automated quality-control rule had removed. Source inventories were frozen and signed before reading, grading was
deterministic, extraction was AI-assisted, and the author reviewed a prespecified subset. \textbf{Results.} Of 797
human single-matrix MS or NMR studies reaching the census independence test, none met the prespecified criteria. No
study in the randomised assessment met the strict one-sample-per-participant standard (0 of 40; one-sided 95\%
hypergeometric upper bound 25 of 371 candidates). Documentation described repeated sampling of the same participants
in 9 of 40 studies (8 on the author's re-review) and cultured cell lines in 3. In 17 of the 19 quality-control-excluded
deposits, other fields identified study samples rather than blanks, 16 of them participant specimens. The author's
review of a prespecified subset disagreed with the AI extraction only in assigning stated counts to the selected
assay, never on the strict result. \textbf{Conclusion.} Under prespecified documentary criteria, participant-level
sampling independence was not established for any study examined. The findings concern what documentation
establishes, not the true independence of deposited samples. Deposition standards should require explicit
participant-to-sample linkage so that dependence can be modelled in reuse and external validation.}

\keywords{metabolomics, data reuse, external validation, metadata, MetaboLights, Metabolomics Workbench,
pseudoreplication, reporting standards}
