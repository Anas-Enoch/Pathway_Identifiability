\title[Identifiability of independent sampling units in public metabolomics data]{Identifiability of independent
sampling units in publicly deposited metabolomics data: a repository-level audit and documentary assessment}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Anas} \sur{Enoch}}\email{anas\_nour@um5.ac.ma}
\affil*[1]{\orgname{Mohammed VI University of Sciences and Health (UM6SS)}, \orgaddress{\city{Casablanca}, \country{Morocco}}}

\abstract{\textbf{Introduction.} Reuse of public metabolomics data requires that independent sampling units be
identifiable; unique sample labels do not identify distinct participants. \textbf{Objectives.} To assess whether
repository metadata and linked documentation establish participant-level sampling structure under prespecified,
affirmative evidence rules. \textbf{Methods.} A metadata-only census of 3,582 public MetaboLights accessions; a
secondary audit of factor metadata in 104 previously selected Metabolomics Workbench studies; a randomised documentary
assessment of 40 of 371 unresolved MetaboLights candidates, reading deposit metadata with linked publications; and a
pre-verification of 19 human deposits removed by an automated quality-control rule. Source inventories were frozen and
signed before reading, grading was deterministic, extraction was AI-assisted, and the author reviewed a prespecified
subset. \textbf{Results.} Of 797 human single-matrix MS or NMR studies reaching the census independence test, none met
the criteria. No randomly selected study met the strict one-sample-per-participant standard (0 of 40; one-sided 95\%
upper bound 25 of 371 candidates). Documentation described repeated sampling in 9 of 40 studies (8 on re-review) and
cell lines in 3. In 17 of 19 quality-control-excluded deposits, other fields identified study samples rather than
blanks. The author's review disagreed with the AI extraction only on assigning counts to the selected assay, never on
the strict result. \textbf{Conclusion.} Under prespecified documentary criteria, participant-level sampling
independence was not established in the census or the randomised assessment. The findings concern what documentation
establishes, not true sample independence. Deposition standards should require explicit participant-to-sample linkage.}
