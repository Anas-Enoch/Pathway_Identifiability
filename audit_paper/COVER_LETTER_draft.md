Dear Editor,

I am submitting the manuscript "Identifiability of independent sampling units in publicly deposited metabolomics data: a repository-level audit and documentary assessment" for consideration as an original research article.

The manuscript asks a narrow question that reuse of public metabolomics data depends on: do deposited metadata and the documentation linked to a deposit allow a secondary analyst to identify the independent sampling units, that is, which samples come from the same participant? Without that information, repeated-measures designs are indistinguishable from cross-sectional ones, and study-level reuse, external validation and meta-analysis risk treating dependent samples as independent. Existing assessments of metabolomics metadata have measured completeness and standards compliance; we are not aware of one that measures this property under a prespecified evidence rule.

The study applies a prespecified, affirmative evidence standard in four complementary analyses, each with its own denominator: a metadata-only census of all 3,582 public MetaboLights accessions available on the census date; a secondary audit of the factor metadata of 104 previously selected Metabolomics Workbench studies; a documentary assessment of a random sample of 40 of 371 unresolved MetaboLights candidates, reading deposit metadata together with the methods sections of linked publications; and a pre-verification of 19 human deposits whose rows an automated quality-control rule had removed in full. Under the strict criterion, no study met the census independence test, and none of the randomly selected studies met the documentary standard (0 of 40; one-sided 95% upper bound of 25 recoverable candidates among 371). Where documentation addressed sampling, it described dependence in 9 of 40 studies and cultured cell lines in 3. The pre-verification found a template-default sample-type label marking every row as a blank in 18 of 19 deposits, where other fields identified study samples in 17 and participant specimens in 16, and a sample-to-assay linkage inconsistency in one deposit. The manuscript is explicit that these findings concern what documentation establishes, not the true independence of deposited samples, and it does not estimate a repository-wide prevalence.

Two aspects of the methods deserve the Editor's attention. First, every analysis was executed under code and protocols fixed by hash and signed before execution, the random sample was drawn with a NIST Randomness Beacon pulse fixed after the lock was signed, and all protocols, code, locks, sealed evidence inventories, evidence records and grades are public in the repository cited in the Data Availability statement. Second, evidence extraction from the documents was AI-assisted (Claude, Anthropic), under fixed written briefs, with every quotation machine-verified against the sealed texts and every contradicting classification adjudicated by the author. The planned independent human validation could not be staffed; the author performed a prespecified re-extraction and a contradiction review instead, and the manuscript discloses the resulting limitations in full (Methods 2.7, Discussion and Supplementary Methods S5). I would rather the Editor and reviewers learn this from the cover letter than from the Methods.

The work is a methodological audit and makes no biological claim. It has not been published or submitted elsewhere. I am the sole author; I have no competing interests and received no specific funding.

The frozen protocols and code are public in the repository named in the Data Availability statement; I would be glad to deposit the documentary-assessment protocol on Protocols.io as well if the journal requires it for a metadata audit.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
