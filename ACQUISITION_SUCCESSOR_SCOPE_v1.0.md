**Date:** 8 November 2026. **Status:** scope and provenance record for review,
consolidating the owner's agreed decisions. This is not a successor protocol,

## 1. Current programme position

- **Closed.** G1-R's prespecified joint criterion is unmet: H1 is supported;
  H2 is not established. H2 is not refuted. The archive remains closed, A0 is
  the comparator, and A2 is not an established challenger. The external
  selector-improvement slot therefore remains empty.
- **Deprioritized, with recorded reasons.** There is no standalone development
  line for positive-gain certificates. The full-covariance region has an exact
  near-tie swap witness; elementary regression F-pivot products have excessive
  central widths; the unknown-variance boundary null gives a 7.42% fixed-pair
  power ceiling at D1's residual degrees of freedom; the published-summary
  plug-in screen leaves about .00034 headroom versus A2's reported .001242
  improvement. These are scoped screens and a priority judgment, not universal
  impossibility or dominance results.
- **Open, with prerequisites.** A new prospective question is average regret
  improvement under a declared condition distribution, subject to an absolute
  expected-harm cap over a declared protected class. Neither its scientific
  budgets nor an executable successor study are settled by the archive.

## 2. The open objective and the declarations it requires

Let theta specify a population and observation experiment. Let R_h(theta) be
expected normalized acquisition regret, including design-sample variation and
any rule randomization. The proposed objective is

    E_(theta~Pi)[R_A0(theta)-R_h(theta)] >= epsilon,
    sup_(theta in Theta_cap)[R_h(theta)-R_A0(theta)] <= tau.

The cap concerns **expected** harm at each protected population. It is distinct
from a per-dataset cap, a confidence-event guarantee, and the former G1-R
relative no-harm criterion. A maximum over a finite grid is not automatically
the displayed supremum over a continuous class.

Before a new executable protocol, declare and externally justify:

1. **Pi and Theta_cap:** the intended condition distribution and protected
   populations, including the scope of any structural constraints.
2. **Loss and normalization:** target units/metric, observation and assay model,
   the regret-to-predictive-gain bridge, and any population versus fitted-
   predictor distinction. Fixed-W Wishart results do not automatically apply
   to a random target-standardization metric or to A0's shrunk score.
3. **epsilon and tau:** absolute scientific benefit and harm budgets, fixed
   before new outcomes. The closed archive must not set these values so that
   a favored candidate passes.
4. **Labels and ties:** candidate-label randomization and the complete tie
   conventions, accounting for A0's existing executable behavior. Do not
   silently edit the frozen comparator to obtain a symmetry theorem.
5. **Success and failure families:** independently specified new structures and
   seeds, including near-optimal baselines, near ties, weak/zero signals,
   variance heterogeneity and relevant conditioning regimes.
6. **Executable validation:** prespecified tuning and comparison rules,
   uncertainty treatment for both objectives, implementation self-tests and
   the established hash-bound, fail-closed execution/integrity boundary.

Two programme scales are already fixed: the **0.10 external relevance factor**,
relative to G1's own measured gain over no measurement, and the **5% assay-
utility/interpretation floor**. The latter is a prospective interpretation/design
choice, not an empirically validated biological threshold. The confirmation and
protocol records are pinned in section 5. These scales do not determine Pi,
Theta_cap, epsilon or tau without the declared predictive-gain bridge. G1-R's
old 0.9/1.1 regret margins are not that bridge.

Candidate families already identified are GCV-tuned decoupled regularization,
the floor-free rule at large training sizes, and possibly a certified rule as
another entrant. None is adopted or newly frozen here. A prespecified adaptive
rule may tune on new training data as declared; archive replay must not select
its thresholds, grids, condition weights or harm budget. The current mathematical
study scope remains Gaussian; an extension would require a separate prospective
declaration.

### Quantitative screens retained with their scope

- The full-covariance swap witness and F-product width screen concern their
  specified constructions at population-center inputs. They do not establish
  uselessness on every random dataset.
- The 7.42% ceiling uses alpha=.05, nu=389, fixed W and an unknown-variance
  Gaussian null. The corresponding full-data oracle extension at n=410 gives
  about 7.49%. Known-variance restrictions or permitted-harm certificates would
  require different calculations. A power ceiling is not zero power or a
  causal decomposition of A2's finite-sample gains.
- N9 uses the versioned D1 summaries, not a new run. Under ordinary six-decimal
  rounding they imply empirical counts 178 / 819 / 3 for candidates 0 / 1 / 2.
  The plug-in relaxation values are about **.000339 (13.17%)** for scatter-based
  certificates and **.000341 (13.26%)** for the full-data oracle convention.
  Thus ".00034, 13.2%-13.3%, about 1.32 times the 10% planning margin" is the
  retained retrospective statement. The rare-tail sampling uncertainty prevents
  treating it as a guaranteed population ceiling or dominance over A2.
- The planning scale for the independent candidate-variance log ratio is
  approximately **2/sqrt(nu)**, not sqrt(2/nu). It is a precision diagnostic,
  not a hard zero-power threshold.
